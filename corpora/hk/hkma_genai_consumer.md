## Principle 1 (Governance and accountability)
The board and senior management of an authorized institution remain accountable for all decisions and processes driven by big data analytics, AI and generative AI. Responsibility cannot be delegated to the model or the vendor.

## Principle 2 (Fairness)
Authorized institutions should ensure that AI-driven decisions and outputs are objective, consistent, ethical and fair to customers, and do not discriminate unfairly against any customer group.

## Principle 3 (Transparency and disclosure)
Authorized institutions should tell customers when a product or service uses AI, and should inform customers that they are interacting with a generative AI system rather than a human. The purpose, use and limitations of the AI should be explained in plain language.

## Principle 4 (Data privacy and protection)
Authorized institutions should implement effective protection measures for customer data used by AI applications, and comply with the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance.

## Principle 5 (Customer-facing generative AI: human in the loop and opt-out)
At the early stage of deploying customer-facing generative AI, authorized institutions should adopt a human-in-the-loop approach so that a person remains responsible for the accuracy of AI-generated output before it reaches customers. Customers should be given the choice to opt out of using generative AI and to request human intervention.
